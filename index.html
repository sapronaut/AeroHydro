Methodology

📐

Structural vibration model

Vibration risk is computed from each zone's base susceptibility, scaled by turbulence category and aircraft type. Aerodynamic flutter at exposed surfaces (fin tip, wing tip) is modeled with a 60% turbulence amplifier.

💧

Pressure load analysis

Hydraulic system pressure (1,500–5,000 PSI) combines with each zone's structural load baseline. Higher pressures magnify risk at already-loaded zones like wing roots and engine pylons.

🌡

Thermal stress scoring

Altitude-driven temperature differentials stress seals and fittings. Engine pylon zones are penalized heavily for proximity to exhaust heat. Scores scale with cruise altitude.

🔧

Maintenance accessibility

Inaccessible junctions cannot be inspected or repaired efficiently. Extreme locations (fin tip, wing tip) score low — a hidden leak in a hard-to-reach spot is a critical safety failure.

🌬

Structural exposure penalty

Zones exposed to direct aerodynamic loads receive a 15-point penalty. The vertical fin tip is a canonical example: it flexes under lateral wind loads, and any junction here risks fatigue cracking.

🧮

Composite score formula

Score = (1 − [Vib×0.30 + Press×0.30 + Therm×0.20 + (1−Access)×0.20 + ExposedPenalty]) × 100

Weights reflect real engineering priorities: vibration and pressure are the dominant failure modes in hydraulic junctions.
